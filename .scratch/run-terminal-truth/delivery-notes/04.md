## (a) Clock behaviour — tests, not a comment grep

The previous “proof” only grepped `src/platform/host-sleep.ts` for the mach_continuous_time comment. That test is **removed**.

### Deterministic simulated clocks (`tests/unit/bot/awake-watchdog.test.ts`)

Injectable `now` + `schedule`. After 5 minutes awake + 30-minute wall jump, remaining idle is 10 minutes of the 15-minute budget (`creditAwakeDelta` caps each fire at one tick).

1. **Continuous overdue tick (macOS-like):** timers keep running; one fire carries a 30-minute delta. `leaves 10 minutes of idle after 5 minutes awake and a 30-minute suspend (late tick)`.
2. **Paused timer then resume (conservative Windows model):** `pause()` during the jump; next fire after `resume()` sees the large delta. `leaves 10 minutes of idle after a wall jump with a paused timer`.

Contract: `creditAwakeDelta(30min) === AWAKE_TICK_MS` (1 s); negative/zero deltas credit 0.

### Observed on this runner (`tests/unit/platform/awake-clock-observation.test.ts`)

Suite title is `awake clocks on ${process.platform}`. While the process is **awake**, `Date.now()`, `process.hrtime.bigint()`, and `setTimeout(40ms)` all advance (≥20 ms). The same file asserts the 30-minute delta cap.

This is **not** a host-suspend experiment. CI machines are not put to sleep.

Simulated shape (1) is that case. macOS CI runs the observation suite while awake. | Host suspend of the GitHub `macos-latest` runner was not performed. |

Sleep never consumes idle or tool grace in the watchdog model because large deltas are capped regardless of whether the platform timer paused or continued. Probe success/failure still does not determine remaining budgets (existing awake-watchdog recovery tests).
